# Product Vision: Commerce Operating System

## One-Line Thesis

**We are building the operating system for physical commerce — starting with the transaction and expanding into every financial and operational decision a small business makes.**

## The Problem

Small physical businesses — starting with UK mobile food vendors — run their operations on a patchwork of disconnected tools: a POS terminal that doesn't talk to their accounting, a spreadsheet for stock, a notepad for recipes, a separate app for staff scheduling, and no real understanding of their unit economics. They know what they sold. They don't know what they *made*.

Existing POS systems (Square, Loyverse, Lightspeed) are transaction recorders. They capture the sale. They don't understand the business. They don't know that a burger costs £2.14 in ingredients, that wastage on lettuce is running 18% above target, that Tuesday lunchtimes at the Shoreditch pitch generate 3x the margin of Saturday markets, or that you should pre-prep 40 fewer portions next week because rain is forecast.

## The Vision

We are not building a better POS. We are building a **commerce intelligence platform** that happens to start with a point-of-sale interface.

The transaction is the entry point. From there, we expand into:

1. **Real-time unit economics** — cost-of-goods-sold per item, per sale, per shift, per location
2. **Inventory intelligence** — ingredient-level stock tracking, automated reorder triggers, waste measurement
3. **Operational automation** — AI-driven prep forecasting, dynamic pricing suggestions, staff scheduling optimisation
4. **Embedded finance** — merchant cash advances underwritten by our own transaction data, automated VAT filing, cash flow forecasting
5. **Multi-location command centre** — a single pane of glass for operators running 2-50 locations

## Why This Wins

**Data moat.** Every transaction enriches our understanding of the business. Over time, we know more about a vendor's economics than they do. That knowledge compounds — across businesses, across categories, across geographies. This is how you build a defensible platform, not a replaceable tool.

**Vertical integration.** Stripe won payments by owning the full stack. We win physical commerce by owning the full operational stack — from the tap on the card reader to the margin on the P&L.

**Offline-first architecture.** Mobile food vendors operate in fields, markets, and festival grounds with unreliable connectivity. Our system works fully offline and syncs when connectivity returns. This is not a feature — it is the foundational architectural constraint that competitors bolt on as an afterthought.

## Beachhead Market

UK mobile food vendors. ~30,000 active businesses. Underserved by existing POS. Price-sensitive but willing to pay for tools that demonstrably improve margin. High word-of-mouth community. Events and markets create natural distribution channels.

## Business Model

- **SaaS subscription**: Tiered by locations and features (£29-£199/mo)
- **Payments processing**: Interchange-plus, targeting 1.5-1.7% blended rate
- **Embedded finance**: Revenue share on lending products (Phase 3+)
- **Data products**: Anonymised, aggregated market intelligence (Phase 4+)

## Long-Term Defensibility

1. Proprietary transaction + operational data across thousands of businesses
2. AI models trained on real cost/revenue/waste data that improve with scale
3. Embedded finance products that create switching costs
4. Network effects from supplier integrations and multi-vendor markets
5. Offline-first architecture that is genuinely hard to replicate well
